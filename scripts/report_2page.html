1. Analysis of the Problem Statement

1.1 Problem Overview

Urban pluvial (flash) flooding is among the most catastrophic, accelerating hazards in modern cities. Extreme convective precipitation overwhelms municipal stormwater drainage, converting roadways, intersections, and pedestrian underpasses into high-velocity open channels within 10 to 30 minutes. This acute crisis impacts four core urban stakeholders: (1) Civilians & Commuters trapped in submerged road corridors without warning; (2) First Responders & 911 Dispatchers routing ambulances into impassable routes due to static, uncalibrated guidance; (3) Municipal Water Authorities lacking continuous, street-scale situational awareness of surcharged culverts; and (4) Critical Public Infrastructure (power substations, hospitals) suffering destructive water ingress.

1.2 Causes and Impact

The crisis stems from compounding physical and architectural vulnerabilities:

  • Accelerating Climate Volatility: Localized convective cloudbursts (>70 mm/h) defy coarse synoptic forecasts.
  • Impervious Surface Expansion: Concrete and asphalt coverage exceeds 85% in urban cores, slashing natural soil infiltration to <10% and accelerating surface runoff hydrograph peaks by 300%–500%.
  • Aging Subsurface Culvert Bottlenecks: Drainage networks sized for historical 10-year storm frequencies suffer hydraulic backwater surcharge during modern 50-year and 100-year cloudburst events.
  • Micro-Topographic Channeling: Minor elevation gradients—such as 15 cm street curbs and barrier walls—dictate runoff corridors, meaning a 10 cm variance drastically alters downstream hazard pathways.

Quantified Socio-Economic Toll: Pluvial urban flooding generates over $50+ Billion USD in annual damages globally. More than 40% of flood-related fatalities occur inside trapped civilian motor vehicles, and emergency response times escalate by up to 320% during unmonitored flash inundation events.

2. Research Gap

2.1 Existing Solutions

Current flood warning technologies are divided into two fundamentally limited paradigms:

  1. Physics-Based Numerical Solvers (SWMM, LISFLOOD-FP, TELEMAC-2D, HEC-RAS): Discretize 2D Saint-Venant shallow water equations via finite-volume methods. While physically conservative, they are computationally intractable—requiring 45 minutes to 6+ hours of compute for a 10 km² catchment at sub-5m resolution, making real-time emergency forecasting impossible.
  2. Standard Black-Box Machine Learning & CNNs: Provide sub-second inference but produce severe physical violations. Lacking conservation laws, they yield "phantom water" or unphysical drying with mass conservation errors of 15%–25%, are locked to fixed grid resolutions, and operate disconnected from live ground sensors.

3. Ideation of the Solution

3.1 Proposed Solution

Crestix Probe delivers a unified cyber-physical platform across three integrated pillars:

  • AI Neural Surrogate: Benchmarked on the official LarNO 1,000-event dataset (UKEA & Futian). Deploys a ConvLSTM-based U-Net predicting continuous water-depth maps across +15 to +60 min horizons in 38.4 ms (AUROC: 0.958, PR-AUC: 0.902), backed by a flagship XGBoost ensemble for 0.45 ms point classification (AUROC: 0.934, F1: 0.875). Saint-Venant mass continuity loss maintains mass error below 0.4%.
  • Autonomous Edge IoT Hardware: Solar-harvesting ESP32 nodes with industrial JSN-SR04T IP67 ultrasonic transducers (±2 mm accuracy, 20–450 cm range, temperature-compensated sound velocity). Dual-path LoRaWAN (12 km) and NB-IoT cellular fallback ensure connectivity during power grid collapse, backed by 180-day LiFePO4 battery reserve.
  • Web Command Cockpit: Next.js 16 / React 19 interactive platform featuring 2D canvas and 3D Three.js terrain rendering, real-time Initial Boundary Condition Controls (t=0m) with 4 presets (Dry, Nominal, Pre-Saturated, Flash Surge), and vehicle-clearance-aware evacuation routing.

3.2 How It Works

5-Stage Transformation Workflow: (1) Geospatial Ingestion: Ingests 0.5m LiDAR DEMs, building GIS footprints, and culvert geometries to compute bed bathymetry and Manning friction tensors. (2) Meteorological Forcing: Streams Doppler radar (NEXRAD) and HRRR forecasts converted via Marshall-Palmer $Z ext{-}R$ relations into space-time hyetographs. (3) Hydrodynamic Ground Truth: Calibrated against high-fidelity 2D Saint-Venant finite volume solutions. (4) Neural Surrogate Inference: ConvLSTM U-Net executes forward inference in 38.4 ms. (5) Closed-Loop IoT Feedback: Ultrasonic readings from ESP32 probes stream via LoRaWAN/WebSockets, dynamically calibrating depth datums and routing graphs.

4. Implementation Roadmap

4.1 Work Completed in 24 Hours

  • Deep Learning Architecture & Benchmarks: Implemented the PyTorch ConvLSTM U-Net for continuous multi-horizon forecasting. Conducted comprehensive empirical evaluation across 1,000 LarNO cases: ConvLSTM U-Net (AUROC: 0.958, PR-AUC: 0.902, F1: 0.884, Latency: 38.4 ms); XGBoost (AUROC: 0.934, PR-AUC: 0.882, F1: 0.875, MAE: 0.024m, Latency: 0.45 ms).
  • Full-Stack Platform Deployment: Deployed live on Render (https://crestix-probe-aqua.onrender.com/) using Next.js 16 (Turbopack), React 19, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS.
  • Initial Simulation Boundary Customizer (t=0m): Interactive boundary sliders for depth ($h_0$), discharge ($Q_0$), and area ($A_0$) with inline telemetry card steppers, transect profile adjustment, and live downstream physical propagation.
  • Hardware Firmware & Testing: Developed ESP32 firmware with temperature compensation ($v = 331.3 + 0.606 \cdot T$), non-blocking acoustic ISR timing, and verified 100% test pass rate across 25 unit test suites (131/131 passing tests) in Vitest.
