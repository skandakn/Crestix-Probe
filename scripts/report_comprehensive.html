1. Analysis of the Problem Statement

1.1 Problem Overview

Urban pluvial (flash) flooding represents one of the most destructive and rapidly escalating hazards in modern cities. Extreme convective precipitation events routinely overwhelm subterranean storm-drainage conduits, converting roadways, intersections, and transit underpasses into high-velocity open channels within 10 to 30 minutes.

This crisis directly impacts four critical stakeholder groups:

  • Urban Commuters & Civilians: Flash inundation submerges vehicles and cuts off residential access routes without actionable lead time.
  • First Responders & Emergency Dispatchers: Ambulances, police, and fire rescue units routinely enter impassable submerged corridors due to delayed static routing, causing critical life-saving delays.
  • Municipal Stormwater Authorities: Drainage operators lack continuous, street-scale visibility into which culverts have surcharged or where detention basins are cresting.
  • Critical Civic Infrastructure: Power distribution substations, hospitals, and water-treatment facilities experience catastrophic electrical and structural water ingress.

1.2 Causes and Impact

The urban flooding crisis is compounded by structural physical drivers:

  • Accelerating Climate Volatility: Cloudbursts generating rainfall intensities exceeding 70 mm/h occur with rising frequency, overwhelming synoptic weather models.
  • Impervious Surface Expansion: Concrete and asphalt cover exceeds 85% in metropolitan centers, reducing natural soil infiltration to <10% and accelerating hydrograph peaks by 300%–500%.
  • Culvert Capacity Deficits: Drainage infrastructure designed for 10-year storm frequencies cannot evacuate 50-year or 100-year convective cloudbursts, triggering severe backwater surcharge.
  • Micro-Topographic Channeling: Minor elevation gradients—such as 15 cm street curbs and barrier walls—dictate runoff corridors, meaning small topographic variations alter downstream hazard trajectories.

Quantified Socio-Economic Toll: Pluvial urban flooding generates over $50+ Billion USD in annual damages globally. More than 40% of flash-flood casualties occur inside trapped civilian motor vehicles, and emergency response times escalate by up to 320% during unmonitored flash inundation events.

2. Research Gap

2.1 Existing Solutions

Existing commercial and academic flood forecasting tools are divided into two distinct paradigms, both of which suffer from structural operational limitations:

  1. Physics-Based Numerical Solvers (SWMM, LISFLOOD-FP, TELEMAC-2D, HEC-RAS):
    Solve 2D Saint-Venant shallow water equations (SWE) via finite-volume or finite-difference numerical discretization schemes over complex terrain meshes. While physically conservative, they are computationally prohibitive—requiring 45 minutes to 6+ hours of compute per storm scenario for a 10 km² catchment at sub-5m resolution, rendering them structurally unusable during an unfolding flash cloudburst.
  2. Standard Black-Box Machine Learning & CNN Surrogates:
    Provide fast sub-second inference but lack physical conservation laws. Unconstrained neural models suffer from severe physical violations, generating "phantom water" (mass creation) or unphysical drying (mass destruction) with mass conservation errors exceeding 15%–25%. Furthermore, they are locked to fixed raster grid resolutions and operate completely disconnected from physical on-ground sensors.

3. Ideation of the Solution

3.1 Proposed Solution

Crestix Probe delivers an end-to-end cyber-physical urban flood intelligence platform consisting of three tightly integrated pillars:

  • Pillar 1: Physics-Informed Neural Surrogate Engine: Trained and benchmarked on the official LarNO 1,000-event benchmark dataset (UKEA & Futian catchments). Features a ConvLSTM-based U-Net predicting continuous water depth across +15, +30, +45, and +60 min horizons in 38.4 ms (AUROC: 0.958, PR-AUC: 0.902), backed by an XGBoost flagship ensemble achieving 0.45 ms inference (AUROC: 0.934, F1: 0.875). Saint-Venant mass continuity loss enforces physical fluid conservation (<0.4% mass error).
  • Pillar 2: Autonomous Edge IoT Sensor Probe: A ruggedized, solar-harvesting edge node powered by an Espressif ESP32 and an industrial JSN-SR04T IP67 ultrasonic transducer (±2 mm precision, 20 cm – 450 cm range) with integrated speed-of-sound temperature compensation. Long-range LoRaWAN (up to 12 km) with Cellular NB-IoT fallback guarantees transmission during telecom grid outages, with 180-day darkness autonomy.
  • Pillar 3: Real-Time Command & Evacuation Cockpit: A production Next.js 16 / React 19 web platform featuring hardware-accelerated 2D canvas and 3D Three.js terrain rendering, real-time Initial Simulation Boundary Condition Controls (t=0 min), and vehicle-clearance-aware evacuation routing.

3.2 How It Works: System Architecture & 5-Stage Workflow

5-Stage Transformation Workflow: (1) Geospatial Ingestion: Ingests 0.5m LiDAR DEMs, building GIS footprints, and culvert geometries to compute bed bathymetry and Manning friction tensors. (2) Meteorological Forcing: Streams Doppler radar (NEXRAD) and HRRR forecasts converted via Marshall-Palmer relations into space-time hyetographs. (3) Hydrodynamic Ground Truth: Calibrated against high-fidelity 2D Saint-Venant finite volume solutions. (4) Neural Surrogate Inference: ConvLSTM U-Net executes forward inference in 38.4 ms. (5) Closed-Loop IoT Feedback: Ultrasonic readings from ESP32 probes stream via LoRaWAN/WebSockets, dynamically calibrating depth datums and routing graphs.
